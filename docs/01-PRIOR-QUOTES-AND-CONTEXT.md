## E. Current Project 2027 proposal — executive handoff

**PROPOSAL:**

- Institutional mission: a lasting public-policy, research and advocacy institution devoted to democratic accountability, constitutional restraint, civil liberties, and the lawful investigation of governmental abuses.
- Organizational inspiration: learn from the Heritage Foundation / Project 2025's operational machinery (policy development, recruitment, training, implementation materials), without adopting its agenda.
- Five proposed divisions:
  1. **Policy Institute** — policy papers, model legislation, budget/constitutional analysis.
  2. **Accountability Office** — FOIA, public evidence repositories, government oversight, legal and forensic peer review.
  3. **Leadership Council** — qualified independent lawyers, historians, public servants, policy experts and researchers.
  4. **Civic Academy** — democratic institutional education and prospective public-servant training.
  5. **Campaign Studio** — publication, documentary, investigative storytelling and public education.
- Two tracks: **2027 congressional oversight and reform**; **2028–2029 prospective executive-branch institutional reform and governance preparation**, contingent on lawful processes and future political developments.
- Proposed initial case study: **Russell Vought and the Office of Management and Budget**, investigating statutory budget authority, executive actions, impoundment-related concerns, FOIA disclosures and legal compliance. This is a proposal to examine evidence, not a concluded finding of wrongdoing.
- Proposed first publication: **Project 2027 Founding Charter**.
- Proposed longer publication: **Mandate for Accountability**.
- Proposed web location: `https://ferventwishes.co/project-2027`.
- Early illustrative metrics: 5 independent reviewers, 3 substantial policy papers, 1 independently auditable investigation. These are proposed targets rather than reported achievements.
- Legal diligence: evaluate whether distinct research/education and lobbying/advocacy entities are appropriate, accounting for 501(c)(3) and 501(c)(4) rules. Seek legal advice before implementation; no entity or tax status has been established by this draft.
