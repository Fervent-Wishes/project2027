A Fervent Wishes Co. public accountability initiative

THE RECORD.

We are building a public case file on potential war crimes in U.S. military operations, including decisions made at the highest levels of command.

We are organizing independent legal, military, forensic, and investigative experts to examine reported strikes, civilian harm, and the actions of senior officials. Our work will rely on original records, FOIA disclosures, verifiable reporting, and legal analysis that other experts can challenge.

Our initial focus is the September 2025 Caribbean vessel strikes and the February 2026 strikes in Minab and Lamerd, Iran. We intend to establish what happened, what was ordered, what officials knew, and which legal standards apply.

The initial investigations

September 2, 2025 · Caribbean Sea

The vessel strikes

U.S. forces struck a vessel suspected of carrying drugs. A second strike killed two survivors of the initial attack. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth defended the follow-on strike, but said he did not see the survivors or watch the second attack. Adm. Frank Bradley told lawmakers there had been no order from Hegseth to kill everyone aboard.

The original footage, operational orders, legal basis for targeting, status of the survivors, and decisions made through the chain of command require scrutiny. So does the question of which body of law governed the operation.

February 28, 2026 · Iran

Minab and Lamerd

In September 2026, a U.N.-mandated fact-finding mission reported reasonable grounds to believe U.S. strikes on a school in Minab and a sports complex in Lamerd constituted war crimes. The report documented extensive civilian casualties. The U.S. has challenged the findings, and U.S. Central Command denied responsibility for Lamerd.

Independent review must examine target intelligence, civilian-harm warnings, weapons selection, precautions taken before the attacks, command approvals, and the findings of any internal U.S. investigation. The U.N. mission's conclusions are investigative findings, not criminal judgments against named individuals.

U.N. fact-finding report · Associated Press reporting

The records we need

We will pursue complete strike footage and metadata, targeting records, mission logs, rules of engagement, legal reviews, communications within the chain of command, after-action reports, civilian-harm assessments, and records of subsequent investigations.

FOIA requests will go to the agencies holding the relevant material, including SOUTHCOM, CENTCOM, the Office of the Secretary of Defense and Joint Staff, and the Defense Inspector General. We will document requests, releases, denials, appeals, and material that remains unavailable.

The legal inquiry will address possible responsibility by Secretary Hegseth and other civilian and military officials. Individual liability depends on evidence of specific acts, authorization, knowledge, and the applicable legal standards. No individual is presumed guilty.

How the review will work

Every published claim will identify its source, date, evidentiary status, and any material disagreement. Primary records will be preserved with their provenance. Expert reviewers will test factual assertions, consider alternative explanations, and assess legal conclusions against the correct framework.

The law of armed conflict does not automatically apply to every military strike. In the Caribbean case, that threshold question is central. The review will also consider relevant international human rights law, domestic law, and military law. Material corrections and dissenting analyses will remain visible in the public record.

Open call · Independent expert peer review

We need your expertise.

We are seeking international humanitarian law specialists, military lawyers, former targeting and operational planners, digital forensics and OSINT analysts, FOIA practitioners, and experienced investigative researchers.

Reviewers will be asked to identify factual errors, examine operational decisions, question our interpretation of evidence, and test the legal analysis. Relevant experience and independence matter. Agreement with our conclusions is not a requirement.
